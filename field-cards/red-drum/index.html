The numbers

Daily bag3 per person per day
Slot20 in minimum · 28 in maximum (total length)
Over-slotUp to 2 over 28 in per license year: one with a Red Drum Tag, one with a Bonus Red Drum Tag. Tagged fish count in addition to your daily bag and possession limit.
Digital tagsOn a digital license, report the fish in the Texas Hunt & Fish app instead of attaching a paper tag.
Source: TPWD Outdoor Annual - Drum bag & length limits - https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/fishing/saltwater-fishing/bag-length-limits/drum-bag-length-limits

Measure it right

The slot is measured as total length: a straight line from the tip of the snout to the extreme tip of the tail fin. A 19½" red goes back. So does a 28½" red - unless you put a tag on it.

Source: TPWD Outdoor Annual - Measurement tips - https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/fishing/measurement-tips

The tags

Red Drum TagCovers one red drum over 28 in per license year. Included at no extra charge with the saltwater fishing endorsement ($10) and with any package that includes it.
Bonus TagCovers one more over-28 in red drum per license year. $3, one per angler per license year, at any license sales location.

Printed license? The moment you keep the fish: fill in every space on the tag, cut out the day and month, and attach the tag with string or wire to the narrowest part of the tail, just ahead of the tail fin.

Sources: TPWD - Fishing endorsements, tags & permits - https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/licenses/fishing-licenses-stamps-tags-packages/fishing-endorsements-and-tags · TPWD - Tagging red drum & spotted seatrout - https://tpwd.texas.gov/regulations/outdoor-annual/fishing/saltwater-fishing/tagging-red-drum

Where reds live

No spot-burning on this card - your spots stay yours. General habitat: redfish work shorelines, grass flats, oyster reefs, and pier and jetty structure. Moving water matters more than any pin on a map, so check the tide and the wind before you pick a shoreline.

When to go

Tendencies, never promises. Reds are catchable year-round in Texas bays. Spring and fall tend to bring the strongest shoreline and marsh action, and from late summer into fall the oversized "bull" reds school along beaches, passes, and jetties.
